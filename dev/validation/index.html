Validation

  • Aligner and k-mer filter: 3,000 random cases each (all placement flags, wildcards, indel costs, error rates) give the same results as cutadapt's Aligner.locate and KmerFinder (test/reference/*.tsv, generated by validation/aligner_reference.py).
  • Whole runs: 41 scenarios on simulated reads (test/cases.jl: every adapter type, linked adapters, --revcomp, error rates, wildcards, actions, quality trimming and filters, demultiplexing single-end, paired-end and combinatorial) produce byte-identical files to cutadapt 5.2 (test/reference/, generated by validation/make_fixtures.jl). During development the same comparison passed on three further seeds with 3,000 reads per file.
  • Real data: an Oxford Nanopore COI sample (502,718 reads, 1.27 Gbp), revcomp, linked anchored primers, discard_untrimmed: the 375,943 reads written (178,814 reverse-complemented) are identical to cutadapt's, line by line.

Speed is a goal, not a guarantee. On that sample (single measurements on a 12-thread Windows laptop): PrimerDemux about 25 s with 4 threads, 52 s with 1, 17 s with an uncompressed output; cutadapt 5.2 (WSL, -j 4) about 25 s. Gzip inputs are decompressed (zlib-ng) by a task of their own while FASTX parses, output blocks are compressed with libdeflate in the worker tasks, and read buffers are reused between batches. With 3 or more threads one is left to the reading tasks. Output size 0.98 GB against cutadapt's 1.02 GB.
